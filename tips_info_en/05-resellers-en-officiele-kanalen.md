Anyone looking for tickets for a sold-out match often ends up on sites that resell tickets. They are easy to find and seem convenient, but there are a few things to know.

## Why are resellers more expensive?

Resellers buy tickets up or offer those of others, and add their own profit and costs on top. For popular matches the price can be a multiple of the club's price. At some matches the difference is small, at others it is enormous.

## What can go wrong?

- The ticket may be in someone else's name and not valid for you.
- The club can cancel tickets that were resold against its rules, so you are refused at the entrance.
- You may buy tickets that are never delivered or that are not genuine.
- If the match is cancelled or moved, a refund is not always arranged.

In some countries there are also legal rules on reselling tickets above face value. So read the terms of the club and the site.

## What are the official alternatives?

- **The club's official ticket site.** There is often a later sale phase for members or for everyone.
- **The club's official resale platform.** Many clubs have their own ticket exchange, where season ticket holders and other buyers can pass on their ticket at the original price.
- **A sign-up or waiting list.** Some clubs send a message when tickets become available.
- **The box office on matchday.** Sometimes tickets are still available just before kick-off. Do not count on it, but it can happen.

## Want to use a reseller anyway?

Then check that the ticket fits the club's rules, compare the total price and choose a party that protects buyers. Preferably pay with a method that lets you claim your money back if something goes wrong.
