## The stadium
The Euroborg (about 22,500 places) opened in 2006 as the successor to the old Oosterpark and is on the southeast side of the city. It is a modern stadium with four covered stands close to the pitch, so the sound hangs well. Behind the goal is the Noordkant, the section for the most fanatical supporters, who stand singing throughout the match. Around the stadium are restaurants and a fan shop, so you can happily spend a few hours there before kick-off.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere in Groningen is warm and raw, and the crowd stands by its club through thick and thin. At matches against the big clubs the stadium is almost full and gets really loud. It pays to arrive an hour early: then you see how the supporters charge up for the match and you can take a look in the fan shop.

## Getting there
The stadium is on the south side of the city, within walking distance of the Stadspark. From Groningen Centraal you walk there in about half an hour; buses also run. By car you can park at surrounding sites, but it is busy on matchdays.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Tickets are generally easy to get, except against big opponents such as Ajax, PSV and Feyenoord. Book in good time then.

## The city
Groningen is a lively student city. Climb the Martinitoren (about 97 metres), visit the Groninger Museum, wander across the Grote Markt and the Vismarkt and stroll through the Noorderplantsoen. The pub and food scene is excellent.

## Why a visit is worth it
FC Groningen is the face of the north of the Netherlands and you notice it everywhere: the club is a community project, with a following that travels from afar and hardly misses a match. The stadium is modern, comfortable and full of atmosphere, without missing the friendliness of a smaller club. Groningen is also a lively student city, so you can easily combine the match with a weekend in town. For anyone looking for an authentic Dutch football day outside the Randstad, this is one of the nicest options.
