At many clubs, the tickets for a match do not go on sale all at once. They are sold in phases, and whoever comes earlier has a better chance. Below you will find the groups you will come across most often. Names and rules differ per club, so always read the explanation on the club's own ticket site.

## Season ticket holders

Season ticket holders usually already have their seat for the whole season. Sometimes they can buy extra tickets for friends or family before sales open to others. If you do not have a season ticket yourself, this phase is usually of no use to you, but you do know when the next phase starts.

## Members

Club members can often buy tickets before the general public. That advantage differs per club: sometimes it is a few hours earlier, sometimes a week. Many clubs have several types of membership, each with its own moment. If you are interested, look at what a membership costs and from when the advantage applies.

## Club card, fan card or supporters' pass

Some clubs require you to have a club card or fan card to be able to buy tickets. This is often not a membership with voting rights, but a pass that lets the club know who is coming. For a visit to such a club, you therefore arrange that pass first, well before the sale starts.

## General sale

In general sale anyone can buy tickets, provided there are still seats. For popular matches, tickets are often almost gone at the start of general sale. For less busy matches there is usually a longer choice.

## What can you expect?

- The more popular the match, the smaller the chance that tickets are still available in general sale.
- A date without a time means the club has not yet announced the time.
- A sale moment can change. So check it once more on the official site shortly before the sale.
