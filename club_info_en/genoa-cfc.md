## The stadium
The Stadio Luigi Ferraris, known as Marassi (about 36,000 places), is in the middle of Genoa among residential areas and is one of the oldest football stadiums in Italy. The stadium has been rebuilt several times and has a characteristic, English-style layout with stands close to the pitch. It is shared with Sampdoria, the city rival. Because of its location between hills and streets, it feels like a stadium that belongs to the city.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Genoa is passionate and proud: the Gradinata Nord sings and waves flags throughout the match. The supporters are famous for their creativity with banners and choreographies. The Derby della Lanterna against Sampdoria is one of the most emotional matches in Italy.

## Getting there
From Genova Brignole station you walk to the stadium in about 15 minutes; buses also run there.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Usually easy to get, except for the big clubs and the derby.

## The city
The Aquarium of Genoa, the Porto Antico, the Palazzi dei Rolli (UNESCO), the narrow alleys (caruggi) and Boccadasse. Genoa is known for pesto and focaccia, and the Cinque Terre is a day trip.

## Why a visit is worth it
Genoa offers one of the most authentic matchday experiences in Italy in a stadium full of character. The atmosphere is intense and local and tickets are generally achievable. Genoa is an underrated port city with narrow alleys, a fine aquarium and excellent focaccia and pesto. The Cinque Terre are close by, so you can make a lovely trip of it.
