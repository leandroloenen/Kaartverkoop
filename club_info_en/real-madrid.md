## The stadium
The Santiago Bernabéu (about 78,000 places) is, after an extensive rebuild, one of the most advanced stadiums in the world. The stadium has a fully retractable roof, a retractable pitch, an enormous circular video screen and a futuristic facade. It stands in the business centre of Madrid, on the Paseo de la Castellana. It is much more than a football stadium: there is a museum with all the trophies, a stadium tour and a visitor centre.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at the Bernabéu is a mixture of grand, refined and demanding: the crowd expects victory and great moments and reacts immediately to everything. On European nights, when the stadium fills up, the experience is among the best you can have in football. Around the stadium are terraces and bars, and you see fans in white everywhere.

## Getting there
Santiago Bernabéu metro station (line 10) is right by the stadium. From Puerta del Sol you are there in about 20 minutes. On matchdays it is very busy; allow plenty of time.

## Buying tickets
Use only the official club site or recognised partners. For top matches it is very difficult. Tickets for lesser opponents or a stadium tour are usually easier.

## The city
The Prado Museum, Reina Sofía, the Royal Palace, Retiro Park, Plaza Mayor, Puerta del Sol and the tapas district La Latina.

## Why a visit is worth it
Real Madrid is one of the most iconic clubs in the world and the stadium is a sight in itself. You see top players in a setting where every match feels like an event, and the tour makes the visit worthwhile even without a match ticket. Madrid is also a buzzing capital with world-class museums, parks and an excellent food culture. For anyone who wants to experience a big European football stadium once, Madrid belongs at the top of the list.
