## The stadium
Camp Nou, the home of FC Barcelona, is one of the largest stadiums in Europe and is being extensively rebuilt and expanded to more than 100,000 places. The stadium is in the Les Corts district and is a symbol of Catalonia. During the rebuilding, parts of the stadium or the matches may have been moved, so check beforehand exactly where the match is being played. There is a club museum, a stadium tour and a large shop.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Barça is international and festive, with supporters from all over the world coming together to see the attacking football. The crowd is demanding but also proud of its own style of play and the La Masia youth academy. Around the stadium it is busy with shops, bars and street vendors.

## Getting there
By metro to Les Corts (line 3) or Collblanc (line 5), and then another 10 to 15 minute walk. Also reachable by bus and tram, about 20 minutes from the centre.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site or official partners. For non-members it is difficult, especially at top matches; use official channels only to avoid scams. A stadium tour with the museum is a good alternative.

## The city
Sagrada Família, Park Güell, Casa Batlló, the Gothic Quarter, La Rambla, Barceloneta beach and Montjuïc.

## Why a visit is worth it
FC Barcelona is more than a football club: it is a symbol of Catalan identity and one of the most admired teams in the world. The combination of a legendary stadium, attacking football and an unmatched club culture makes a visit a special experience. Barcelona is also one of the most attractive cities in Europe, with Gaudí architecture, beach, excellent restaurants and a lively nightlife. Anyone who wants to see one match there effectively gets a complete city trip thrown in.
