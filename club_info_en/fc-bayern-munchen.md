## The stadium
The Allianz Arena (about 75,000 places) opened in 2005 on the northern edge of Munich and is world famous for its illuminated facade that can light up red, blue or white. The stadium has three tiers of steep stands and a good view from almost every seat. There is an extensive club museum, the FC Bayern Erlebniswelt, and you can book a tour. The Südkurve is the heart of the supporters' section.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Bayern is big, professional and often imposing. The stadium is almost always sold out and the crowd comes in large numbers, in red and white. The level of the football is high and matches against Dortmund or European heavyweights are real events.

## Getting there
Take the U6 from Marienplatz to Fröttmaning, in about 20 minutes. The stadium is on the northern edge of the city.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Many places are for members and season ticket holders; for single tickets you have to be early. Use official channels only.

## The city
The Marienplatz, the Frauenkirche, the Viktualienmarkt, the Englischer Garten, the Deutsches Museum and the Hofbräuhaus. In September and October there is the Oktoberfest.

## Why a visit is worth it
Bayern München has been the dominant club in Germany for decades and you see top players in a modern and imposing stadium. The combination with a city visit to Munich is strong: the old town, the Biergärten, the museums and the Alps are close by. It is also an excellent base for a football trip through southern Germany. Anyone who wants to see the best of German football starts here.
