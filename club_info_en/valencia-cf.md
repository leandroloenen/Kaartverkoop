## The stadium
Mestalla (about 49,000 places) is one of the oldest and most recognisable stadiums in Spain and stands in the middle of Valencia, within walking distance of the centre. The stadium has extremely steep stands, so the crowd almost hangs over the pitch and the sound is amplified. It is known for its intimidating character and its old-fashioned look. A new stadium has been under construction for years; so check beforehand whether the match is still being played at Mestalla.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Mestalla is particularly passionate, with a crowd that is demanding and reacts immediately to what happens. The steep stands amplify every chant and every whistling concert. Around the stadium are many bars where you can eat and drink before the match, and the atmosphere in the streets is festive.

## Getting there
The stadium is in the city. From the centre you are there on foot in about 30 minutes, or take the metro to Aragón or the tram.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Usually easy to get, except against Madrid, Barcelona and top matches.

## The city
The City of Arts and Sciences, the Mercado Central, La Lonja, the Turia gardens, Malvarrosa beach and the Albufera nature park. Valencia is the cradle of paella; in March there are the Fallas.

## Why a visit is worth it
Valencia CF plays in a stadium with plenty of character, in a city that offers both modern architecture and old culture. The crowd is proud and passionate, and a match at Mestalla stays with you for a long time. Valencia also has an excellent climate, beaches, a buzzing food scene and the cradle of paella. Anyone who wants to combine football and a city trip in a city that is less overrun than Barcelona or Madrid makes a smart choice here.
