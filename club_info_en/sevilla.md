## The stadium
Estadio Ramón Sánchez-Pizjuán (about 43,000 places) is in the Nervión district, within walking distance of the centre of Seville. The stadium has steep stands with a roof, so the crowd sits close to the pitch and the sound is held in. You see the club's red and white colours everywhere. It is an intimate stadium for its size and has an old-fashioned, classic atmosphere.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Sevilla is particularly fierce and proud: the supporters sing loudly, wave scarves and turn it into a party. On European nights and at the Gran Derbi against Real Betis the experience is unmatched. Around the stadium are bars and terraces that fill up before the match and where everyone walks around in the club colours.

## Getting there
The stadium is about 2 km from the centre and can be reached on foot in 20 to 30 minutes. Santa Justa station is close by, from where you can also walk.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Demand is high; for the Gran Derbi, Madrid and Barcelona it is very difficult. For other matches it is usually doable.

## The city
The Real Alcázar, the cathedral and the Giralda, Plaza de España, the Triana and Santa Cruz districts, flamenco and tapas. Well known are Semana Santa and the Feria de Abril.

## Why a visit is worth it
Sevilla is one of the nicest football cities in Europe, with a club used to European success and a stadium full of atmosphere. The match is passionate and the city itself offers flamenco, tapas, palaces and sunny squares. You can visit the Alcázar and the cathedral during the day and go to the match in the evening. Anyone looking for a football trip with a southern character will find a perfect destination in Seville.
