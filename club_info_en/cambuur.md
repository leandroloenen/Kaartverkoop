## The stadium
The Cambuur Stadion (about 10,500 places) opened in 2007 on the south side of Leeuwarden and is the club's home ground. It is a compact, modern stadium with four covered stands right next to the pitch. The club's yellow and blue colours are everywhere. The stadium is clear and practical, with a good view from every spot and enough food and drink outlets.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Cambuur is proud, Frisian and remarkably fanatical for the size of the stadium. The crowd sings throughout the match and the supporters feel every moment. At the Frisian derby against Heerenveen the whole province is on edge and the stadium is filled to the last seat.

## Getting there
The stadium is about 3 km from Leeuwarden station. You take the bus or cycle; walking is also possible, in about 35 minutes. By car you can park nearby.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. The stadium is small, so tickets for big matches and the Frisian derby are hard to get. For other matches it is usually achievable, but look in good time.

## The city
Leeuwarden has the Oldehove (a leaning tower), the Fries Museum, the Princessehof and the birthplace of Mata Hari. The city was European Capital of Culture in 2018.

## Why a visit is worth it
Cambuur is a club with a large and loyal following in a small stadium, and that makes for an intimate and loud matchday experience. Tickets are generally affordable and the atmosphere is warm. Leeuwarden is a beautiful, compact city with canals, museums and its own Frisian culture, and the Wadden coast and the lakes are close by. Anyone who wants to get to know the north of the Netherlands is well placed here.
