## The stadium
The Stadio Diego Armando Maradona (about 54,000 places) is in the Fuorigrotta district, in the west of Naples. The stadium was built for the 1990 World Cup and was named after Maradona following his death. The stands are tall and steep, but the athletics track creates some distance from the pitch. Around the stadium are murals of Maradona and many references to the legend. The stadium is a place of pilgrimage for football fans.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Napoli is overwhelming and emotional: the whole stadium sings, waves flags and trembles. The Curva A and Curva B are known for their passion and choreographies. You feel that football in Naples is almost a religion, and the match is an unforgettable experience.

## Getting there
Take metro line 2 to Campi Flegrei and walk about 10 minutes. The Cumana train to Mostra also runs.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Very difficult: matches are almost always sold out and you need ID. Use official channels only.

## The city
Pizza (the origin), Spaccanapoli, Castel dell'Ovo, the National Archaeological Museum, Cappella Sansevero. Pompeii, Vesuvius and Capri are close by.

## Why a visit is worth it
Napoli offers the most intense stadium atmosphere in Italy and a city that feels its club as its soul. The match is often a feast of singing and colour. Naples itself is raw, lively and beautiful, with the best pizza in the world, and Pompeii and Vesuvius close by. Tickets are hard to get, but those who succeed experience something special.
