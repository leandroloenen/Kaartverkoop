## The stadium
Signal Iduna Park (about 81,000 places) is the largest stadium in Germany and is in the south of Dortmund, in a green sports park. The most famous part is the Südtribüne, the "Yellow Wall", a yellow wall of some 25,000 standing places behind one goal. The stadium has four tall, steep stands and is one of the most impressive football stadiums in the world. There is a club museum and you can book a tour.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere in Dortmund is overwhelming: the Südtribüne sings throughout the match and the stadium is almost always sold out. Before kick-off the streets around the stadium fill with supporters drinking beer and eating sausages. The feeling of being part of something big is hard to match.

## Getting there
Take U-Bahn U42 from Dortmund Hbf to Westfalenhallen (about 10 to 15 minutes). With your match ticket you often travel free in the region on matchdays.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Demand is enormous and season ticket holders take up many places. For single tickets you need luck or good planning; avoid unofficial sales.

## The city
The German Football Museum, Dortmunder U, Westfalenpark with the Florianturm, the Reinoldikirche and the Zeche Zollern.

## Why a visit is worth it
Borussia Dortmund is seen by many as having the best stadium atmosphere in Europe and it is one of the great football experiences you can ever have. The stadium is huge, the crowd fanatical and the football often attacking and spectacular. Tickets are hard to get, but with preparation it can be done. Anyone who wants to see one German match usually chooses Dortmund, and rightly so.
