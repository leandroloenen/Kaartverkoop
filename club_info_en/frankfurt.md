## The stadium
The Deutsche Bank Park (about 58,000 places) is in the Frankfurter Stadtwald, a large wooded area south of the centre. The stadium has a striking roof with a substantial canopy and a retractable roof section. The Nordwestkurve behind the goal is one of the most fanatical stands in Germany. The stadium sits next to a large sports complex and is well equipped for big matches.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Eintracht Frankfurt is impressive: lots of flags, big banners and a crowd that sings throughout the match. The supporters are known for their sold-out stadium and their love of travel, both at home and in Europe. On European nights the stadium is a seething, singing mass.

## Getting there
By S-Bahn (S7, S8, S9) from the Hbf to Stadion, about 10 minutes, or by tram.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Many matches are sold out, so it is very difficult. Plan early and stick to official channels.

## The city
The Römer, the skyline along the Main, the Städel Museum, the Goethe House, Sachsenhausen with apple wine (Apfelwein) and the Kleinmarkthalle.

## Why a visit is worth it
Eintracht Frankfurt has one of the best supporters' cultures in Europe and a stadium that fits it completely. The match is often spectacular and the atmosphere overwhelming. Frankfurt itself offers a modern skyline, museums and the well-known apple wine. Tickets are hard to get, but those who succeed experience one of the best football nights in Germany.
