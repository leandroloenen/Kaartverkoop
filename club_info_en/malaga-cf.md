## The stadium
La Rosaleda (about 30,000 places) is Málaga's home ground and sits on the edge of the centre, close to the Guadalmedina river. The stadium dates from 1941 and has been modernised over time, but has kept its classic character. The stands are relatively close to the pitch and the club's blue and white colours dominate. On clear days you can see the mountains behind the stadium.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Málaga is southern, warm and friendly, with a loyal crowd that keeps supporting its club, even in less good times. Matchdays are often sunny and the streets around the stadium are filled with people eating tapas and having a beer. It is a relaxed and accessible way to experience Spanish football.

## Getting there
The stadium is about 2 km from the centre. You walk there in about 25 minutes, or take the bus. Málaga María Zambrano station is within walking distance. The airport is about 10 km outside the city.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Usually easy to get.

## The city
The Alcazaba and Gibralfaro castle, the Roman theatre, the cathedral, the Picasso Museum (Picasso was born here), the Centre Pompidou and the beaches of the Costa del Sol. Eat espetos (grilled sardines).

## Why a visit is worth it
Málaga combines a classic stadium with one of the most popular cities on the Costa del Sol. You visit a match in a friendly atmosphere and can enjoy the beach, the old town and the museums during the day. Tickets are affordable and the city is easy to reach by plane. For a football weekend with sun, sea and culture this is an excellent choice.
