## The stadium
The Hill Dickinson Stadium (about 52,000 places) is Everton's new home and sits at Bramley-Moore Dock, on the bank of the Mersey. The stadium is built in a historic dock and has a brick and glass facade that refers to the industrial past of the harbour. The stands are steep and close to the pitch. Its location on the water makes it one of the most remarkable new stadiums in England.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Everton is proud, humorous and loyal: the crowd feels every moment and keeps singing, even in adversity. The move to the new stadium has brought new energy. The Merseyside derby against Liverpool is one of the most emotional matches in England.

## Getting there
Take the train to Sandhills (Merseyrail), or a bus from Liverpool Lime Street. From the centre you can walk in about 30 minutes along the waterfront.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Many places go to members, and the Merseyside derby is very difficult. For other matches there are sometimes single tickets.

## The city
Albert Dock, The Beatles Story, the Cavern Club, the Royal Liver Building, the cathedrals and the Mersey Ferry.

## Why a visit is worth it
Everton has a rich club culture and a new stadium that is special for its location on the water. The crowd is friendly and proud of the club, even though it has been hard for a while. Liverpool offers plenty of sights, music and a welcoming population. Anyone who wants to see the new stadium and taste Merseyside culture is in the right place here.
