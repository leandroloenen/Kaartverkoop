Buying football tickets for a match abroad

Visiting a match abroad is a great experience, but buying the tickets takes a bit more preparation. These are the things you can look out for.

Start early

Foreign clubs sometimes put tickets on sale months in advance. At the big clubs, tickets for the top matches go quickly. So when planning your trip, first check when sales start, before you book your flight and hotel.

Check whether you need a membership or club card

In some countries a tourist can only buy tickets with a membership or club account, in others it is simply possible as a guest. On the club pages of this site you can see per club whether a condition applies. Read the club's own explanation for the current rules.

Language and payment

Most ticket shops have an English version, but not all of them. If needed, use your browser's translation function. Note that foreign payment methods sometimes do not work with a Dutch debit card. A credit card is often the safest choice.

Time and time zones

Times on club sites are sometimes given in local time. On this site we convert them to Dutch time. When setting a reminder, check that the time matches your own time zone. Sales often open at a fixed hour and popular matches then sell out quickly.

Kick-off often not yet known

For league matches, the exact kick-off is often only fixed a few weeks in advance. So preferably book travel and hotel flexibly. At the weekend a match can be played on Saturday, Sunday or sometimes even Monday.

Transport and tickets

At some clubs your match ticket also counts as a travel pass. In a number of German cities, for example, you can use public transport to the stadium for free with your ticket. That helps, but check it in advance on the club's site.

On matchday

  • Bring ID.
  • Make sure you have a charged phone with your tickets on it.
  • Arrive in good time, as security can be strict.
  • Read the rules on bags and other items.
