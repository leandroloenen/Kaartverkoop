## The stadium
The Stadium of Light (about 49,000 places) opened in 1997 on the bank of the River Wear, on the site of a former coal mine. The name refers to the miners' lamps, and a large miner's lamp stands close to the entrance as a tribute. The stadium has steep, red stands and an imposing layout. It is one of the largest stadiums in northeast England and feels big, yet intimate.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Sunderland is raw, proud and loud: the crowd sings throughout the match and fills the stadium even in difficult times. The Roker End provides plenty of singing. The Tyne-Wear derby against Newcastle is one of the fiercest in England.

## Getting there
Take the Metro to Stadium of Light, or walk in about 10 to 15 minutes from Sunderland station. From Newcastle it is about 40 minutes.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Usually easy to get; for the derby against Newcastle it is difficult.

## The city
Roker Beach with the lighthouse, the National Glass Centre, Penshaw Monument and Souter Lighthouse. Durham with its cathedral is close by.

## Why a visit is worth it
Sunderland is a true working-class and community club with an enormous following, a big stadium and an impressive atmosphere. The match is often easy to attend and affordable, and the crowd is friendly and welcoming. The region offers coastlines, castles and Durham Cathedral. For anyone who wants to experience real English football outside the big cities, this is a good choice.
