## The stadium
Borussia-Park (about 54,000 places) opened in 2004 on the edge of Mönchengladbach and replaced the old Bökelberg stadium. The stadium has four covered stands and steep slopes, so you have a good view everywhere. In the immediate area there is plenty of parking, and there is a fan shop and a club museum. It is a modern stadium with plenty of atmosphere and space.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Gladbach is warm, loyal and loud. The Nordkurve sings a lot and the crowd supports the club regardless of the score. The stadium offers a traditional German matchday experience, with beer, sausage and lots of singing.

## Getting there
The stadium is on the edge of the city. On matchdays there are special shuttles and buses from the Hbf. You are there in about 20 minutes.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. It is usually achievable, but against Bayern, Dortmund and Köln it is difficult.

## The city
Mönchengladbach is not a tourist city, but has the Museum Abteiberg, Schloss Rheydt and the Münster. Combine with Düsseldorf or Cologne.

## Why a visit is worth it
Borussia Mönchengladbach is a traditional club with a large and loyal crowd in a modern stadium. Tickets are generally easy to get and matchday is friendly. The city itself is not big, but is close to Düsseldorf and Cologne, so you can easily make a weekend of it. For anyone who wants to see classic German football without the big crowds, this is a good choice.
