## The stadium
The Veltins-Arena (about 62,000 places) opened in 2001 and is one of the most modern stadiums in Germany. The stadium has a retractable roof and a pitch that can be slid outside, so the grass can grow in the open air. The Nordkurve behind the goal is one of the largest and most fanatical standing terraces in the country. Schalke's blue and white colours dominate the whole stadium.

## The matchday experience
The atmosphere at Schalke is working-class proud, emotional and loud. The crowd sings, jumps and lives the match completely. On matchdays supporters come in large numbers from all over the Ruhr area to the stadium, and at the Revierderby against Dortmund the tension is almost unbearable.

## Getting there
By tram line 302 from Gelsenkirchen Hbf directly to the Arena, in about 15 to 20 minutes.

## Buying tickets
Through the official club site. Usually easy to get. The Revierderby against Dortmund is the hardest.

## The city
Gelsenkirchen is industrial. Of interest are the Zeche Zollverein in Essen (UNESCO), Nordsternpark and ZOOM Erlebniswelt. The Ruhr area has plenty of industrial heritage.

## Why a visit is worth it
Schalke offers one of the most authentic matchday experiences in Germany: a large stadium, a fanatical crowd and a club that embodies the Ruhr area. The match is often well attended and affordable, and the atmosphere makes up for a lot. Gelsenkirchen is not a tourist city, but the Ruhr area has plenty of industrial heritage and combines well with Dortmund and Essen. Anyone who wants to experience real German football chooses Schalke.
